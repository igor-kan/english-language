1. The Rotational Geometry of “Turn”

From Old English tyrnan (to turn, rotate, wheel, revolve), turn denotes directional rotation. In phrasal combinations, this rotational dynamic shifts metaphorically to represent rejection of offers, radical reversals of fortune, metamorphic transformations, and ideological betrayals.

[ Spectrum 1: Rejection vs. Acceptance ]       ──> turn down       <──> accept / embrace
[ Spectrum 2: Transformation & Metamorphosis ] ──> turn into       <──> preserve / remain
[ Spectrum 3: Reversal of Fortune ]            ──> turn around     <──> deteriorate / collapse
[ Spectrum 4: Loyalty vs. Defection ]          ──> turn against    <──> rally behind / support
[ Spectrum 5: Emergence vs. Absence ]          ──> turn up         <──> vanish / fail to appear

2. Spectrum 1: Rejection vs. Acceptance

A. Turn Down

  • Sense 1 (Rejection of Offer/Request): To reject, refuse, or decline an application, proposal, invitation, or candidate.
    • Single-word Equivalent: Decline, reject, rebuff, spurn.
    • Polar Opposite: Accept, embrace, ratify, adopt.
    • Example: “The brilliant researcher turned down lucrative private equity offers to pursue fundamental research in mathematical physics.”
  • Sense 2 (Volume/Intensity Attenuation): Reduce the volume, heat, or brightness of a device (“turn down the volume”; antonym: turn up).

3. Spectrum 2: Metamorphosis and Evolution

A. Turn Into

  • Core Definition: To change, metamorphose, or be transformed completely into something different.
    • Single-word Equivalent: Metamorphose, transmute, convert, evolve into.
    • Polar Opposite: Remain immutable, preserve integrity.
    • Example: “What began as a minor disagreement over code formatting quickly turned into an acrimonious theological flame war.”

4. Spectrum 3: Reversals of Fortune and Revitalization

A. Turn Around

  • Sense 1 (Economic / Organizational Revitalization): To take a failing, insolvent, or stagnant enterprise and transform it into a profitable, successful operation.
    • Single-word Equivalent: Revitalize, resuscitate, rehabilitate.
    • Polar Opposite: Run into the ground, mismanage into insolvency.
    • Example: “The new CEO turned around the struggling semiconductor manufacturer within three quarters by canceling unviable speculative projects.”
  • Sense 2 (Inversion of Logic): To reinterpret an opponent’s argument to work against them (“turn the tables”).

5. Spectrum 4: Defection, Betrayal, and Loyalty

A. Turn Against

  • Core Definition: To stop supporting, liking, or trusting someone and become hostile or adversarial toward them.
    • Single-word Equivalent: Defect, betray, turn hostile, repudiate.
    • Polar Opposite on Spectrum: Rally behind, stand by, champion, remain loyal.
    • Example: “When the public relations crisis erupted, the board of directors quickly turned against the founder they had lionized weeks earlier.”
    • Idiom on Spectrum: Stab in the back / bite the hand that feeds you.

6. Spectrum 5: Eventuation, Attendance, and Emergence

A. Turn Out

  • Sense 1 (Eventuation / Final Result): To develop, happen, or end in a specific way.
    • Single-word Equivalent: Eventuate, transpire, resolve.
    • Example: “Despite our early anxieties about memory usage, the deployment turned out to be remarkably stable.”
  • Sense 2 (Public Attendance): For people to assemble or attend an event in a collective crowd (“over ten thousand people turned out for the rally”).
  • Sense 3 (Production): To manufacture or produce goods (“the foundry turns out five thousand ingots a day”).

B. Turn Up

  • Sense 1 (Fortuitous Discovery): To be discovered or found, often unexpectedly, after being lost (“the missing contract turned up in the archives”).
  • Sense 2 (Arrival): To arrive, especially unexpectedly or after a delay (“he turned up two hours late to the defense”).
