1. Introduction: Semasiology vs. Onomasiology in Phrasal Verb Mastery

For centuries, learners of English have fallen into what linguists call “The Dictionary Trap” (the semasiological approach). A student takes a single base verb—such as look, take, make, or come—and reads an alphabetical catalog of its particle combinations:

[Traditional Semasiological Approach] (Word-to-Meaning)
"LOOK" ──► look after, look down on, look for, look forward to, look into, look up...
(Cognitively disconnected; creates massive semantic interference and confusion)

When you study phrasal verbs this way, your brain attempts to memorize dozens of unrelated meanings attached to a single phonetic root. In high-stakes speech or rapid academic writing, retrieval fails because the brain cannot untangle the arbitrary particles (into, over, up, through, after) attached to the same base verb.

In this educational masterclass, we employ the Onomasiological (Concept-First) Approach:

[Onomasiological Semantic Field Approach] (Meaning-to-Words)
"INVESTIGATION" ──► look into, probe into, go over, sift through, check out, dig into
"VIGILANCE"     ──► look out for, watch out for, keep an eye out for, guard against
"DEFERENCE"     ──► look up to, bow to, defer to, lean on, stand in awe of
"SCORN"         ──► look down on, talk down to, look down one's nose at
(Builds intuitive, networked neural schemas based on communicative intention)

By organizing phrasal verbs into functional semantic fields, you learn words according to the intent of the speaker. Furthermore, this guide systematically covers every major phrasal verb of “look”, embedding each within its broader ecological family alongside verbs of come, go, make, take, get, put, set, bring, run, and turn.

2. Semantic Domain 1: Investigation, Scrutiny & Empirical Inspection

When an analytical thinker needs to examine a document, investigate a crime, or probe an ambiguous dataset, English deploys a finely calibrated family of phrasal verbs balancing speed, depth, and skepticism.

                    SPECTRUM OF SCRUTINY & EXAMINATION
  [Superficial / Rapid]                                   [Exhaustive / Forensic]
  look over ──► check out ──► look through ──► sift through ──► look into / probe into

Core Verb Entries & Nuances

1. Look into (Inseparable: look into + Object)

  • Semantic Core: Formal, official, or systemic investigation into a problem, crime, or discrepancy.
  • Register: High formal, journalistic, legal, corporate.
  • Contextual Example: “The parliamentary ethics committee announced it would look into the allegations of improper lobbying.”
  • Cross-Verbal Partners:
    • Probe into: Deeper, more penetrating scrutiny against active resistance (“journalists probed into the ministry’s offshore accounts”).
    • Dig into: Tenacious, gritty investigative effort (“the auditor dug into ten years of tax filings”).

2. Look over vs. Look through vs. Go over

  • Look over (Separable): A rapid, preliminary inspection to assess general quality or detect obvious flaws.
    • “Could you look over my draft memorandum before I send it to the board?”
  • Look through (Inseparable): Reading or scanning through a collection of documents or items, looking for specific entries.
    • “I looked through the registry but found no record of the patent.”
  • Go over (Inseparable): Methodical, step-by-step review, checking for precision, errors, or mutual comprehension.
    • “Let us go over the contract clause by clause to ensure no ambiguities remain.”

3. Sift through & Pore over

  • Sift through: Sorting through a large, messy volume of data to isolate valuable or incriminating evidence.
    • “Forensic accountants spent weeks sifting through thousands of encrypted transactions.”
  • Pore over: Absorbing text with intense, prolonged intellectual concentration.
    • “The scholar spent decades poring over medieval ecclesiastical manuscripts.”

3. Semantic Domain 2: Vigilance, Caution & Warning

Language in this domain alerts listeners to imminent physical danger, warns of strategic vulnerabilities, or urges continuous awareness.

                    THE VIGILANCE SPECTRUM
  [Immediate Physical Hazard]                  [Strategic Continuous Monitoring]
  "Look out!" / "Watch out!" ────────► keep an eye out for ──► guard against

Core Verb Entries & Nuances

1. Look out (for) & Watch out (for)

  • Look out! (Intransitive Interjection): Urgent imperative shouted to avert immediate physical accident.
    • “Look out! The scaffold above is swaying!”
  • Look out for (Three-part Inseparable): Maintaining active alertness for a potential hazard, person, or opportunity over time.
    • “When negotiating with aggressive venture funds, look out for onerous liquidation preferences.”
  • Watch out for: Nearly identical to look out for, but often carries a stronger connotation of calculated defensive prudence against predatory actors.

2. Cross-Verbal Partners in Vigilance

  • Keep an eye out for: Informal to neutral vigilance; remaining observant while engaged in other activities.
    • “Please keep an eye out for the courier while I finish this phone call.”
  • Look sharp: Idiomatic imperative meaning to act quickly and stay mentally alert.
    • “Look sharp, team; the inspectors will arrive in fifteen minutes.”
  • Guard against: Elevated formal verb denoting preventive structural measures taken against systemic risks.
    • “Central banks must guard against imported inflationary pressures.”
  • Steer clear of: Active, conscious avoidance of a dangerous person, topic, or pitfall.
    • “Diplomats were instructed to steer clear of contentious territorial questions.”

4. Semantic Domain 3: Social Deference, Stature & Scorn

Human social hierarchies, moral estimation, and status judgments are deeply encoded in vertical spatial metaphors (UP = good, noble, high status; DOWN = inferior, contemptible, base).

                      VERTICAL METAPHORS OF STATURE

                                [HIGH STATUS]
                                     ▲
                                     │  look up to / hold in awe
                                     │  (Admiration, moral deference)
                         ════════════╪════════════ [NEUTRAL LEVEL]
                                     │
                                     │  look down on / talk down to
                                     ▼  (Arrogance, contempt, condescension)
                                [LOW STATUS]

Core Verb Entries & Nuances

1. Look up to (Three-part Inseparable)

  • Semantic Core: Respecting, admiring, and viewing someone as an ethical or professional exemplar.
  • Syntactic Frame: Subject + look up to + Object.
  • Contextual Example: “Generations of young jurists looked up to Justice Marshall as a paragon of constitutional integrity.”
  • Companion Verbs:
    • Stand in awe of: Feeling overwhelming reverence or intimidation toward someone’s genius or power.
    • Bow to / Defer to: Submitting respectfully to superior authority or expertise (“The minister bowed to the scientific consensus”).

2. Look down on & Look down one’s nose at (Three-part Inseparable)

  • Semantic Core: Regarding someone or something as socially, intellectually, or morally inferior; contemptuous haughtiness.
  • Contextual Example: “The aristocratic elite habitually looked down on self-made industrial entrepreneurs.”
  • Idiomatic Reinforcement: Look down one’s nose at (emphasizes aristocratic physical posture of contempt).
    • “He looks down his nose at anyone who did not attend an ancient university.”
  • Companion Verbs:
    • Talk down to: Speaking in a condescending, patronizing manner that assumes the listener is stupid or childlike (“Do not talk down to your constituents”).

5. Semantic Domain 4: Care, Supervision & Guardianship

This semantic field governs taking responsibility for living beings, institutional projects, or operational logistics.

                    CARE & SUPERVISION METAPHORS
  [Living Beings / Protective Custody]          [Logistics / Task Execution]
  look after ──► watch over                  see to ──► attend to

Core Verb Entries & Nuances

1. Look after (Inseparable)

  • Semantic Core: Providing ongoing physical, emotional, or financial care for someone (children, elderly parents, pets, health), or managing property responsibly.
  • Contextual Example: “While her brother was deployed overseas, she looked after his estate and investments.”
  • Nuance Contrast with “Watch over”:
    • Look after implies active practical care (cooking, maintenance, bills).
    • Watch over implies protective, vigilant guardianship from a higher position (“The elder sister watched over the young boy on the crowded playground”).

2. See to & Attend to (Task & Logistics Guardianship)

  • See to (Inseparable): Taking direct personal responsibility for getting a specific task completed or an issue resolved.
    • “I have a meeting at three, but I will see to the catering arrangements first thing tomorrow.”
    • Pattern: See to it that [Clause]: Ensure that something happens (“Please see to it that the security protocols are enforced”).
  • Attend to (Formal Inseparable): Dealing officially or professionally with a client, problem, or chore.
    • “The emergency physician was forced to attend to three trauma patients simultaneously.”
  • Fend for (oneself): Surviving independently without external care or guardianship.
    • “Cast out into the capital without money, the young apprentice was left to fend for himself.”

6. Semantic Domain 5: Anticipation, Expectation & Reliance

How speakers look forward in time, place reliance upon future events, or count on collaborators.

                     TEMPORAL & RELIANCE PROJECTIONS
  [Positive Temporal Eagerness]                 [Structural Dependency / Faith]
  look forward to ──► look ahead             count on ──► bank on ──► look to

Core Verb Entries & Nuances

1. Look forward to (Three-part Inseparable)

  • Semantic Core: Anticipating a future event with genuine pleasure and eager expectation.
  • Critical Syntactic Trap: The word to here is a preposition, NOT an infinitive particle. It must be followed by a noun phrase or a gerund (-ing):
    • Incorrect: “I look forward to hear from you.”
    • Correct: “I look forward to hearing from you.”
    • “The research team looks forward to presenting its findings at the symposium.”

2. Look to (Inseparable: look to + Object + for + Noun / to + Verb)

  • Semantic Spectrum:
    1. Seeking guidance, inspiration, or leadership: “In times of acute fiscal turbulence, citizens look to the central bank for decisive leadership.”
    2. Intending / Planning to do something (Formal): “The enterprise looks to expand into Southeast Asian territories next spring.”

3. Companion Verbs of Reliance

  • Count on / Bank on: Placing firm financial or emotional reliance upon an outcome.
    • “We cannot bank on interest rates dropping before the debt refinance matures.”
  • Bargain for (Negative / Modal Contexts): Expecting or anticipating an obstacle.
    • “When she accepted the cabinet appointment, she got far more controversy than she had bargained for.”
  • Look ahead (Intransitive): Thinking strategically about future possibilities rather than dwelling on the past.
    • “Rather than lamenting the quarterly deficit, management urged the directors to look ahead.”

7. Semantic Domain 6: Information Retrieval, Search & Discovery

Expressing the deliberate or accidental discovery of facts, objects, and people.

                      MODALITIES OF RETRIEVAL
  [Database / Reference Index]               [Physical Environment / Exploration]
  look up (in a lexicon/record)              look for / hunt down / seek out
                                    │
                                    ▼ [Serendipity]
                       come across / stumble upon / hit upon

Core Verb Entries & Nuances

1. Look up (Separable)

  • Semantic Core: Consulting a reference work (dictionary, encyclopedia, database, directory) to retrieve a specific, isolated datum.
  • Contextual Example: “If you are uncertain of the statutory limitation period, look it up in the civil procedure code.”
  • Idiomatic Extension (Intransitive: Things are looking up): Conditions, fortunes, or prospects are noticeably improving.
    • “After six consecutive quarters of contraction, retail sales figures are finally looking up.”

2. Look for vs. Seek out vs. Track down

  • Look for (Inseparable): The baseline term for searching for an item or person whose location is unknown.
  • Seek out (Separable): Deliberately making an effort to find and engage with a specific, high-value person or resource.
    • “Ambitious postdocs should actively seek out mentors with strong grant-writing records.”
  • Track down / Hunt down (Separable): Finding an elusive or fleeing person, rare document, or missing artifact after an arduous pursuit.
    • “The archivist spent three years tracking down the missing volumes of Newton’s personal correspondence.”
  • Ferret out (Separable): Unearthing secrets, hidden scandals, or concealed funds through persistent digging.
    • “Investigative reporters ferreted out the offshore trusts used to conceal political donations.”

3. Serendipitous Discovery: Come across & Hit upon

  • Come across (Inseparable): Discovering something or encountering someone purely by chance.
    • “While browsing an antiquarian bookstore, she came across a signed first edition of Joyce.”
  • Hit upon / Strike upon (Inseparable): Suddenly conceiving a brilliant idea, strategy, or solution after prolonged struggle.
    • “After months of dead-ends, the lab team finally hit upon a stable catalytic compound.”

8. Semantic Domain 7: Retrospection, Memory & Deliberation

How the human intellect reflects upon past events or deliberates over complex choices.

                    RETROSPECTION & DELIBERATION
  [Evaluating the Past]                         [Active Cognitive Deliberation]
  look back on ──► think back to             mull over ──► turn over ──► chew over

Core Verb Entries & Nuances

1. Look back on (Three-part Inseparable)

  • Semantic Core: Reviewing, reflecting upon, and evaluating past periods of life, historical decisions, or past careers from a position of temporal distance.
  • Contextual Example: “In his twilight years, the retired statesman looked back on his foreign policy achievements with pride, tempered by regret over missed diplomatic opportunities.”
  • Companion: Think back to: Recalling specific memories of a past era (“Think back to where this company was a decade ago”).

2. Cognitive Deliberation Verbs

  • Mull over / Think over (Separable): Pondering a major life decision, proposal, or offer deeply and unhurriedly.
    • “The board asked for forty-eight hours to mull over the hostile takeover bid.”
  • Turn over (in one’s mind) (Separable): Considering a difficult thought or scenario from multiple angles.
    • “She spent the entire flight turning over the implications of the prosecutor’s warning.”

9. Semantic Domain 8: Perception, Resemblance & Social Presentation

How objects appear, how individuals present themselves, and how genetic or stylistic likeness is inherited.

                      PERCEPTION & RESEMBLANCE
  [Sensory Discerning]                       [Genetic / Personal Likeness]
  make out (discern silhouette)              take after (resemble ancestor)
                                    │
                                    ▼ [Public Social Projection]
                        come across as / pass for / pose as

Core Verb Entries & Nuances

1. Look like vs. Take after

  • Look like: Superficial physical resemblance or probability (“It looks like rain”).
  • Take after (Inseparable: take after + Person): Inheriting physical appearance, psychological temperament, or mannerisms from an ancestor (parent, grandparent).
    • “With her sharp mathematical intellect and fierce determination, she clearly takes after her grandmother.”
    • Crucial Distinction: You cannot say “He takes after his younger brother” (must be an older relative).

2. Come across (as) vs. Pass for

  • Come across as: The spontaneous psychological or social impression one projects to observers.
    • “Under intense television lights, the candidate came across as cold, defensive, and unapproachable.”
  • Pass for (Inseparable): Being accepted as something one might not truly be, due to plausible surface traits.
    • “His French accent was so impeccable that he could easily pass for a native Parisian.”
  • Pose as (Inseparable): Intentionally pretending to be someone else for deceitful purposes (“posed as an insurance investigator”).

10. Semantic Domain 9: Tolerance, Condonation & Overlooking

The cognitive and ethical act of enduring hardship or consciously ignoring an infraction.

                    TOLERANCE & CONDONATION
  [Enduring Discomfort / Abuse]                 [Conscious / Unconscious Neglect]
  put up with ──► bear with                  overlook ──► wink at ──► pass over

Core Verb Entries & Nuances

1. Look over vs. Overlook (A Perilous Lexical Contrast!)

Notice how swapping the particle transforms the entire meaning: * Look over: To deliberately inspect and examine something (“She looked over the contract”). * Overlook (Single-word Verb): 1. Unintentional oversight: Failing to notice an error or detail (“The editor overlooked a glaring typographical error on page four”). 2. Intentional condonation: Consciously choosing to forgive, ignore, or indulge a minor offense (“In light of his exemplary record, the headmaster resolved to overlook the student’s minor curfew violation”).

2. Put up with & Bear with

  • Put up with (Three-part Inseparable): Enduring, tolerating, or accepting an unpleasant person, behavior, or condition without complaining.
    • “I cannot understand why the department puts up with his persistent arrogance and tardiness.”
  • Bear with (Inseparable Imperative): Asking someone to be patient while you resolve a difficulty.
    • “Please bear with us while our technical engineers reboot the central server.”
  • Wink at (Inseparable Formal): Pretending not to see an illegal or corrupt act; tacitly condoning wrongdoing.
    • “Corrupt port authorities routinely winked at the smuggling of contraband luxury goods.”

11. Semantic Domain 10: Essential Distillation & Ultimate Causation

Expressing how complex webs of argument or circumstance reduce to a singular decisive point.

                 THE DISTILLATION FUNNEL
  [Dispersed Complex Data] ──► come down to / boil down to ──► [Core Truth]

Core Entries

  • Come down to (Three-part Inseparable): Distilling a complex issue to its essential, deciding core.
    • “When stripped of rhetorical posturing, the negotiation simply came down to cash valuation.”
  • Boil down to (Three-part Inseparable): Identical in meaning to come down to, rooted in culinary distillation (evaporating excess liquid until the pure essence remains).
    • “The entire twenty-page brief boils down to a single constitutional question.”
  • Hinge on / upon (Inseparable): The outcome of a massive event depends entirely on a small pivot.
    • “The defendant’s freedom hinges upon whether the DNA evidence is ruled admissible.”

12. Semantic Domain 11: Causation, Genesis & Triggering

How events are provoked, initiated, or brought into existence.

                    GENESIS & TRIGGERING
  [Deliberate Orchestration]                 [Explosive Triggering / Ignition]
  bring about ──► give rise to              set off ──► spark off ──► touch off

Core Entries

  • Bring about (Separable): Causing something significant or systemic to happen over time.
    • “The discovery of the transistor brought about an unprecedented technological revolution.”
  • Set off / Spark off / Touch off (Separable): Triggering a sudden, explosive, or violent chain reaction.
    • “The sudden currency devaluation set off widespread panic across global bond markets.”
    • “The assassination of the archduke touched off the First World War.”
  • Give rise to (Formal Three-part): Producing or causing new intellectual theories, doubts, or phenomena.
    • “The unexpected experimental anomalies gave rise to a flurry of rival theoretical models.”

13. Semantic Domain 12: Cessation, Abandonment & Postponement

Terminating, winding down, or delaying actions and projects.

                    TERMINATION & DELAY
  [Definitive Cancellation]   [Phased Wind-Down]    [Postponement / Delay]
  call off                     wind up / phase out   put off

Core Entries

  • Call off (Separable): Canceling an event, strike, search, or military operation that had already been scheduled or begun.
    • “Due to torrential monsoons, the mountaineers were forced to call off the summit assault.”
  • Put off (Separable): Postponing or delaying an appointment or obligation to a later date.
    • “Do not put off filing your foreign tax disclosure until the final statutory deadline.”
  • Wind up (Separable & Intransitive):
    1. Liquidating / Closing an entity: Bringing a business, project, or meeting to an orderly close (“The liquidator was appointed to wind up the insolvent airline”).
    2. Ending up in a state/place: Finding oneself in an unintended condition (“If you persist with this reckless speculation, you will wind up bankrupt”).
  • Phase out (Separable): Gradually, systematically withdrawing or eliminating a product, fuel, or policy over time.
    • “The European Union agreed to phase out internal combustion passenger vehicles by 2035.”

14. Semantic Domain 13: Compensation, Reconciliation & Restitution

Restoring balance, compensating for past losses, or healing fractured interpersonal relationships.

                    RECONCILIATION & COMPENSATION
  [Compensating for Deficits]                 [Restoring Personal Harmony]
  make up for                                 make up with / patch up

Core Entries

  • Make up for (Three-part Inseparable): Providing something valuable to compensate for, offset, or atone for a loss, error, or deficiency.
    • “Her extraordinary work ethic and structural discipline made up for her lack of prior banking experience.”
  • Make up with (Three-part Inseparable): Reconciling and restoring friendly relations after a bitter quarrel or estrangement.
    • “After a decade of icy silence, the two founding partners finally made up with each other.”
  • Patch up (Separable): Hastily or temporarily repairing a damaged relationship or a flawed plan.
    • “The diplomats managed to patch up a fragile ceasefire before the winter snows set in.”

This master table allows you to look up any communicative intent and instantly retrieve its full phrasal verb ecosystem:

Formal Investigation Look into, Probe into Inseparable (into + Obj) Official, legal, journalistic. “The inspector general will look into the procurement audit.”
Rapid Inspection Look over, Check out Separable (look [Obj] over) Preliminary, checking obvious flaws. “Please look over these draft galleys before printing.”
Detailed Review Go over, Pore over Inseparable (over + Obj) Thorough, meticulous, academic. “Let us go over the treaty clauses once more.”
Urgent Warning Look out, Watch out Intransitive / Imperative Immediate physical danger. “Look out! The cargo sling has snapped!”
Continuous Vigilance Look out for, Guard against Inseparable (for / against + Obj) Strategic, defensive caution. “Investors must look out for subtle liquidity traps.”
Moral Admiration Look up to, Hold in awe Three-part Inseparable Hierarchical respect, mentorship. “She always looked up to her doctoral advisor.”
Contemptuous Scorn Look down on, Talk down to Three-part Inseparable Snobbery, social condescension. “It is vulgar to look down on blue-collar labor.”
Practical Guardianship Look after, Watch over Inseparable (after / over + Obj) Ongoing custody, estate care. “He looked after his younger siblings during the war.”
Task Execution See to, Attend to Inseparable (to + Obj) Logistical responsibility. “The chief of staff will see to the security briefing.”
Eager Anticipation Look forward to Three-part (to + Gerund/Noun) Future pleasure (requires -ing). “We look forward to collaborating on this initiative.”
Seeking Guidance Look to Inseparable (to + Person + for) Dependence on authority. “Citizens look to the judiciary to uphold rights.”

16. Formative Diagnostic & Contextual Practice

Solidify your command of this semantic taxonomy by choosing the most precise phrasal verb for each high-register scenario:

Question 1

The independent commission was appointed by Parliament to ________ allegations of systemic bribery inside the defense procurement ministry. * (A) look over * (B) look into * (C) look up * (D) look after

Question 2

Although the junior associate lacked formal training in corporate bankruptcy, her formidable analytical stamina and mathematical precision ________ her initial inexperience. * (A) came across * (B) made off with * (C) made up for * (D) looked down on

Question 3

The university senate voted to establish an endowed chair in behavioral neuroscience, emphasizing that its research agenda would serve as a catalyst and ________ for the school’s medical expansion. * (A) look forward * (B) springboard * (C) put up with * (D) call off

Question 4

I would be deeply grateful if you could ________ my draft appellate brief before tomorrow morning’s filing, simply to confirm there are no glaring citation errors. * (A) look over * (B) overlook * (C) look down on * (D) look up to

Question 5

Senior research fellows were strictly reminded never to ________ junior laboratory technicians, whose daily procedural diligence kept the automated sequencers operating smoothly. * (A) look after * (B) look down on * (C) look forward to * (D) look into

Question 6

We eagerly ________ with your delegation during next month’s international monetary conference in Geneva. * (A) look forward to meet * (B) look forward to meeting * (C) look ahead to have met * (D) look up to meeting

Question 7

The regional power grid operator urged municipal authorities to implement rolling brownouts to ________ an unmitigated catastrophic blackout. * (A) guard against * (B) see to * (C) pass for * (D) look back on

Question 8

While auditing sixty thousand rows of inventory data, the junior compliance officer accidentally ________ a series of illicit transfers disguised as routine shipping rebates. * (A) looked up to * (B) came across * (C) looked down on * (D) looked after

Comprehensive Explanatory Answer Key

  • 1. (B) Look into — Formal, systemic, or official investigation of an allegation, crime, or institutional failure is expressed by look into. (A) Look over means a quick, superficial check; (C) Look up means checking reference material.
  • 2. (C) Made up for — In the sense of compensating or counterbalancing a deficiency or deficit.
  • 3. (B) Springboard — Used in its propulsive metaphorical sense: an active launchpad that propels future expansion.
  • 4. (A) Look over — Reviewing a document rapidly to spot obvious errors or assess quality. (B) Overlook is a dangerous distractor meaning to fail to notice or to intentionally ignore an infraction.
  • 5. (B) Look down on — Treating subordinates or colleagues with condescension or social scorn.
  • 6. (B) Look forward to meeting — The grammatical rule is absolute: in look forward to, the particle to is a preposition, mandating a gerund (-ing) (meeting), not a bare infinitive.
  • 7. (A) Guard against — Taking strategic, preventive countermeasures against a systemic disaster.
  • 8. (B) Came across — Discovering anomalous evidence or hidden entries through serendipitous, unplanned encounter.

## 1. Introduction: Semasiology vs. Onomasiology in Phrasal Verb Mastery

For centuries, learners of English have fallen into what linguists call **"The Dictionary Trap"** (the *semasiological* approach). A student takes a single base verb—such as **look**, **take**, **make**, or **come**—and reads an alphabetical catalog of its particle combinations:

```
[Traditional Semasiological Approach] (Word-to-Meaning)
"LOOK" ──► look after, look down on, look for, look forward to, look into, look up...
(Cognitively disconnected; creates massive semantic interference and confusion)
```

When you study phrasal verbs this way, your brain attempts to memorize dozens of unrelated meanings attached to a single phonetic root. In high-stakes speech or rapid academic writing, retrieval fails because the brain cannot untangle the arbitrary particles (*into*, *over*, *up*, *through*, *after*) attached to the same base verb.

In this educational masterclass, we employ the **Onomasiological (Concept-First) Approach**:

```
[Onomasiological Semantic Field Approach] (Meaning-to-Words)
"INVESTIGATION" ──► look into, probe into, go over, sift through, check out, dig into
"VIGILANCE"     ──► look out for, watch out for, keep an eye out for, guard against
"DEFERENCE"     ──► look up to, bow to, defer to, lean on, stand in awe of
"SCORN"         ──► look down on, talk down to, look down one's nose at
(Builds intuitive, networked neural schemas based on communicative intention)
```

By organizing phrasal verbs into **functional semantic fields**, you learn words according to the **intent of the speaker**. Furthermore, this guide systematically covers **every major phrasal verb of "look"**, embedding each within its broader ecological family alongside verbs of *come, go, make, take, get, put, set, bring, run, and turn*.

## 2. Semantic Domain 1: Investigation, Scrutiny & Empirical Inspection

When an analytical thinker needs to examine a document, investigate a crime, or probe an ambiguous dataset, English deploys a finely calibrated family of phrasal verbs balancing speed, depth, and skepticism.

```
                    SPECTRUM OF SCRUTINY & EXAMINATION
  [Superficial / Rapid]                                   [Exhaustive / Forensic]
  look over ──► check out ──► look through ──► sift through ──► look into / probe into
```

### Core Verb Entries & Nuances

#### 1. Look into (Inseparable: look into + Object)
* **Semantic Core:** Formal, official, or systemic investigation into a problem, crime, or discrepancy.
* **Register:** High formal, journalistic, legal, corporate.
* **Contextual Example:** *"The parliamentary ethics committee announced it would **look into** the allegations of improper lobbying."*
* **Cross-Verbal Partners:**
  * **Probe into:** Deeper, more penetrating scrutiny against active resistance (*"journalists **probed into** the ministry's offshore accounts"*).
  * **Dig into:** Tenacious, gritty investigative effort (*"the auditor **dug into** ten years of tax filings"*).

#### 2. Look over vs. Look through vs. Go over
* **Look over (Separable):** A rapid, preliminary inspection to assess general quality or detect obvious flaws.
  * *"Could you **look over** my draft memorandum before I send it to the board?"*
* **Look through (Inseparable):** Reading or scanning through a collection of documents or items, looking for specific entries.
  * *"I **looked through** the registry but found no record of the patent."*
* **Go over (Inseparable):** Methodical, step-by-step review, checking for precision, errors, or mutual comprehension.
  * *"Let us **go over** the contract clause by clause to ensure no ambiguities remain."*

#### 3. Sift through & Pore over
* **Sift through:** Sorting through a large, messy volume of data to isolate valuable or incriminating evidence.
  * *"Forensic accountants spent weeks **sifting through** thousands of encrypted transactions."*
* **Pore over:** Absorbing text with intense, prolonged intellectual concentration.
  * *"The scholar spent decades **poring over** medieval ecclesiastical manuscripts."*

## 3. Semantic Domain 2: Vigilance, Caution & Warning

Language in this domain alerts listeners to imminent physical danger, warns of strategic vulnerabilities, or urges continuous awareness.

```
                    THE VIGILANCE SPECTRUM
  [Immediate Physical Hazard]                  [Strategic Continuous Monitoring]
  "Look out!" / "Watch out!" ────────► keep an eye out for ──► guard against
```

### Core Verb Entries & Nuances

#### 1. Look out (for) & Watch out (for)
* **Look out! (Intransitive Interjection):** Urgent imperative shouted to avert immediate physical accident.
  * *"**Look out!** The scaffold above is swaying!"*
* **Look out for (Three-part Inseparable):** Maintaining active alertness for a potential hazard, person, or opportunity over time.
  * *"When negotiating with aggressive venture funds, **look out for** onerous liquidation preferences."*
* **Watch out for:** Nearly identical to *look out for*, but often carries a stronger connotation of calculated defensive prudence against predatory actors.

#### 2. Cross-Verbal Partners in Vigilance
* **Keep an eye out for:** Informal to neutral vigilance; remaining observant while engaged in other activities.
  * *"Please **keep an eye out for** the courier while I finish this phone call."*
* **Look sharp:** Idiomatic imperative meaning to act quickly and stay mentally alert.
  * *"**Look sharp**, team; the inspectors will arrive in fifteen minutes."*
* **Guard against:** Elevated formal verb denoting preventive structural measures taken against systemic risks.
  * *"Central banks must **guard against** imported inflationary pressures."*
* **Steer clear of:** Active, conscious avoidance of a dangerous person, topic, or pitfall.
  * *"Diplomats were instructed to **steer clear of** contentious territorial questions."*

## 4. Semantic Domain 3: Social Deference, Stature & Scorn

Human social hierarchies, moral estimation, and status judgments are deeply encoded in vertical spatial metaphors (UP = good, noble, high status; DOWN = inferior, contemptible, base).

```
                      VERTICAL METAPHORS OF STATURE

                                [HIGH STATUS]
                                     ▲
                                     │  look up to / hold in awe
                                     │  (Admiration, moral deference)
                         ════════════╪════════════ [NEUTRAL LEVEL]
                                     │
                                     │  look down on / talk down to
                                     ▼  (Arrogance, contempt, condescension)
                                [LOW STATUS]
```

### Core Verb Entries & Nuances

#### 1. Look up to (Three-part Inseparable)
* **Semantic Core:** Respecting, admiring, and viewing someone as an ethical or professional exemplar.
* **Syntactic Frame:** *Subject + look up to + Object*.
* **Contextual Example:** *"Generations of young jurists **looked up to** Justice Marshall as a paragon of constitutional integrity."*
* **Companion Verbs:**
  * **Stand in awe of:** Feeling overwhelming reverence or intimidation toward someone's genius or power.
  * **Bow to / Defer to:** Submitting respectfully to superior authority or expertise (*"The minister **bowed to** the scientific consensus"*).

#### 2. Look down on & Look down one's nose at (Three-part Inseparable)
* **Semantic Core:** Regarding someone or something as socially, intellectually, or morally inferior; contemptuous haughtiness.
* **Contextual Example:** *"The aristocratic elite habitually **looked down on** self-made industrial entrepreneurs."*
* **Idiomatic Reinforcement:** **Look down one's nose at** (emphasizes aristocratic physical posture of contempt).
  * *"He **looks down his nose at** anyone who did not attend an ancient university."*
* **Companion Verbs:**
  * **Talk down to:** Speaking in a condescending, patronizing manner that assumes the listener is stupid or childlike (*"Do not **talk down to** your constituents"*).

## 5. Semantic Domain 4: Care, Supervision & Guardianship

This semantic field governs taking responsibility for living beings, institutional projects, or operational logistics.

```
                    CARE & SUPERVISION METAPHORS
  [Living Beings / Protective Custody]          [Logistics / Task Execution]
  look after ──► watch over                  see to ──► attend to
```

### Core Verb Entries & Nuances

#### 1. Look after (Inseparable)
* **Semantic Core:** Providing ongoing physical, emotional, or financial care for someone (children, elderly parents, pets, health), or managing property responsibly.
* **Contextual Example:** *"While her brother was deployed overseas, she **looked after** his estate and investments."*
* **Nuance Contrast with "Watch over":**
  * *Look after* implies active practical care (cooking, maintenance, bills).
  * **Watch over** implies protective, vigilant guardianship from a higher position (*"The elder sister **watched over** the young boy on the crowded playground"*).

#### 2. See to & Attend to (Task & Logistics Guardianship)
* **See to (Inseparable):** Taking direct personal responsibility for getting a specific task completed or an issue resolved.
  * *"I have a meeting at three, but I will **see to** the catering arrangements first thing tomorrow."*
  * *Pattern: **See to it that [Clause]**:* Ensure that something happens (*"Please **see to it that** the security protocols are enforced"*).
* **Attend to (Formal Inseparable):** Dealing officially or professionally with a client, problem, or chore.
  * *"The emergency physician was forced to **attend to** three trauma patients simultaneously."*
* **Fend for (oneself):** Surviving independently without external care or guardianship.
  * *"Cast out into the capital without money, the young apprentice was left to **fend for himself**."*

## 6. Semantic Domain 5: Anticipation, Expectation & Reliance

How speakers look forward in time, place reliance upon future events, or count on collaborators.

```
                     TEMPORAL & RELIANCE PROJECTIONS
  [Positive Temporal Eagerness]                 [Structural Dependency / Faith]
  look forward to ──► look ahead             count on ──► bank on ──► look to
```

### Core Verb Entries & Nuances

#### 1. Look forward to (Three-part Inseparable)
* **Semantic Core:** Anticipating a future event with genuine pleasure and eager expectation.
* **Critical Syntactic Trap:** The word **to** here is a **preposition**, NOT an infinitive particle. It must be followed by a **noun phrase** or a **gerund (-ing)**:
  * *Incorrect:* *"I look forward to hear from you."*
  * *Correct:* *"I **look forward to hearing** from you."*
  * *"The research team **looks forward to presenting** its findings at the symposium."*

#### 2. Look to (Inseparable: look to + Object + for + Noun / to + Verb)
* **Semantic Spectrum:**
  1. *Seeking guidance, inspiration, or leadership:* *"In times of acute fiscal turbulence, citizens **look to** the central bank for decisive leadership."*
  2. *Intending / Planning to do something (Formal):* *"The enterprise **looks to expand** into Southeast Asian territories next spring."*

#### 3. Companion Verbs of Reliance
* **Count on / Bank on:** Placing firm financial or emotional reliance upon an outcome.
  * *"We cannot **bank on** interest rates dropping before the debt refinance matures."*
* **Bargain for (Negative / Modal Contexts):** Expecting or anticipating an obstacle.
  * *"When she accepted the cabinet appointment, she got far more controversy than she had **bargained for**."*
* **Look ahead (Intransitive):** Thinking strategically about future possibilities rather than dwelling on the past.
  * *"Rather than lamenting the quarterly deficit, management urged the directors to **look ahead**."*

## 7. Semantic Domain 6: Information Retrieval, Search & Discovery

Expressing the deliberate or accidental discovery of facts, objects, and people.

```
                      MODALITIES OF RETRIEVAL
  [Database / Reference Index]               [Physical Environment / Exploration]
  look up (in a lexicon/record)              look for / hunt down / seek out
                                    │
                                    ▼ [Serendipity]
                       come across / stumble upon / hit upon
```

### Core Verb Entries & Nuances

#### 1. Look up (Separable)
* **Semantic Core:** Consulting a reference work (dictionary, encyclopedia, database, directory) to retrieve a specific, isolated datum.
* **Contextual Example:** *"If you are uncertain of the statutory limitation period, **look it up** in the civil procedure code."*
* **Idiomatic Extension (Intransitive: Things are looking up):** Conditions, fortunes, or prospects are noticeably improving.
  * *"After six consecutive quarters of contraction, retail sales figures are finally **looking up**."*

#### 2. Look for vs. Seek out vs. Track down
* **Look for (Inseparable):** The baseline term for searching for an item or person whose location is unknown.
* **Seek out (Separable):** Deliberately making an effort to find and engage with a specific, high-value person or resource.
  * *"Ambitious postdocs should actively **seek out** mentors with strong grant-writing records."*
* **Track down / Hunt down (Separable):** Finding an elusive or fleeing person, rare document, or missing artifact after an arduous pursuit.
  * *"The archivist spent three years **tracking down** the missing volumes of Newton's personal correspondence."*
* **Ferret out (Separable):** Unearthing secrets, hidden scandals, or concealed funds through persistent digging.
  * *"Investigative reporters **ferreted out** the offshore trusts used to conceal political donations."*

#### 3. Serendipitous Discovery: Come across & Hit upon
* **Come across (Inseparable):** Discovering something or encountering someone purely by chance.
  * *"While browsing an antiquarian bookstore, she **came across** a signed first edition of Joyce."*
* **Hit upon / Strike upon (Inseparable):** Suddenly conceiving a brilliant idea, strategy, or solution after prolonged struggle.
  * *"After months of dead-ends, the lab team finally **hit upon** a stable catalytic compound."*

## 8. Semantic Domain 7: Retrospection, Memory & Deliberation

How the human intellect reflects upon past events or deliberates over complex choices.

```
                    RETROSPECTION & DELIBERATION
  [Evaluating the Past]                         [Active Cognitive Deliberation]
  look back on ──► think back to             mull over ──► turn over ──► chew over
```

### Core Verb Entries & Nuances

#### 1. Look back on (Three-part Inseparable)
* **Semantic Core:** Reviewing, reflecting upon, and evaluating past periods of life, historical decisions, or past careers from a position of temporal distance.
* **Contextual Example:** *"In his twilight years, the retired statesman **looked back on** his foreign policy achievements with pride, tempered by regret over missed diplomatic opportunities."*
* **Companion: Think back to:** Recalling specific memories of a past era (*"Think back to where this company was a decade ago"*).

#### 2. Cognitive Deliberation Verbs
* **Mull over / Think over (Separable):** Pondering a major life decision, proposal, or offer deeply and unhurriedly.
  * *"The board asked for forty-eight hours to **mull over** the hostile takeover bid."*
* **Turn over (in one's mind) (Separable):** Considering a difficult thought or scenario from multiple angles.
  * *"She spent the entire flight **turning over** the implications of the prosecutor's warning."*

## 9. Semantic Domain 8: Perception, Resemblance & Social Presentation

How objects appear, how individuals present themselves, and how genetic or stylistic likeness is inherited.

```
                      PERCEPTION & RESEMBLANCE
  [Sensory Discerning]                       [Genetic / Personal Likeness]
  make out (discern silhouette)              take after (resemble ancestor)
                                    │
                                    ▼ [Public Social Projection]
                        come across as / pass for / pose as
```

### Core Verb Entries & Nuances

#### 1. Look like vs. Take after
* **Look like:** Superficial physical resemblance or probability (*"It looks like rain"*).
* **Take after (Inseparable: take after + Person):** Inheriting physical appearance, psychological temperament, or mannerisms from an **ancestor** (parent, grandparent).
  * *"With her sharp mathematical intellect and fierce determination, she clearly **takes after** her grandmother."*
  * *Crucial Distinction:* You cannot say *"He takes after his younger brother"* (must be an older relative).

#### 2. Come across (as) vs. Pass for
* **Come across as:** The spontaneous psychological or social impression one projects to observers.
  * *"Under intense television lights, the candidate **came across as** cold, defensive, and unapproachable."*
* **Pass for (Inseparable):** Being accepted as something one might not truly be, due to plausible surface traits.
  * *"His French accent was so impeccable that he could easily **pass for** a native Parisian."*
* **Pose as (Inseparable):** Intentionally pretending to be someone else for deceitful purposes (*"posed as an insurance investigator"*).

## 10. Semantic Domain 9: Tolerance, Condonation & Overlooking

The cognitive and ethical act of enduring hardship or consciously ignoring an infraction.

```
                    TOLERANCE & CONDONATION
  [Enduring Discomfort / Abuse]                 [Conscious / Unconscious Neglect]
  put up with ──► bear with                  overlook ──► wink at ──► pass over
```

### Core Verb Entries & Nuances

#### 1. Look over vs. Overlook (A Perilous Lexical Contrast!)
Notice how swapping the particle transforms the entire meaning:
* **Look over:** To deliberately inspect and examine something (*"She **looked over** the contract"*).
* **Overlook (Single-word Verb):**
  1. *Unintentional oversight:* Failing to notice an error or detail (*"The editor **overlooked** a glaring typographical error on page four"*).
  2. *Intentional condonation:* Consciously choosing to forgive, ignore, or indulge a minor offense (*"In light of his exemplary record, the headmaster resolved to **overlook** the student's minor curfew violation"*).

#### 2. Put up with & Bear with
* **Put up with (Three-part Inseparable):** Enduring, tolerating, or accepting an unpleasant person, behavior, or condition without complaining.
  * *"I cannot understand why the department **puts up with** his persistent arrogance and tardiness."*
* **Bear with (Inseparable Imperative):** Asking someone to be patient while you resolve a difficulty.
  * *"Please **bear with** us while our technical engineers reboot the central server."*
* **Wink at (Inseparable Formal):** Pretending not to see an illegal or corrupt act; tacitly condoning wrongdoing.
  * *"Corrupt port authorities routinely **winked at** the smuggling of contraband luxury goods."*

## 11. Semantic Domain 10: Essential Distillation & Ultimate Causation

Expressing how complex webs of argument or circumstance reduce to a singular decisive point.

```
                 THE DISTILLATION FUNNEL
  [Dispersed Complex Data] ──► come down to / boil down to ──► [Core Truth]
```

### Core Entries
* **Come down to (Three-part Inseparable):** Distilling a complex issue to its essential, deciding core.
  * *"When stripped of rhetorical posturing, the negotiation simply **came down to** cash valuation."*
* **Boil down to (Three-part Inseparable):** Identical in meaning to *come down to*, rooted in culinary distillation (evaporating excess liquid until the pure essence remains).
  * *"The entire twenty-page brief **boils down to** a single constitutional question."*
* **Hinge on / upon (Inseparable):** The outcome of a massive event depends entirely on a small pivot.
  * *"The defendant's freedom **hinges upon** whether the DNA evidence is ruled admissible."*

## 12. Semantic Domain 11: Causation, Genesis & Triggering

How events are provoked, initiated, or brought into existence.

```
                    GENESIS & TRIGGERING
  [Deliberate Orchestration]                 [Explosive Triggering / Ignition]
  bring about ──► give rise to              set off ──► spark off ──► touch off
```

### Core Entries
* **Bring about (Separable):** Causing something significant or systemic to happen over time.
  * *"The discovery of the transistor **brought about** an unprecedented technological revolution."*
* **Set off / Spark off / Touch off (Separable):** Triggering a sudden, explosive, or violent chain reaction.
  * *"The sudden currency devaluation **set off** widespread panic across global bond markets."*
  * *"The assassination of the archduke **touched off** the First World War."*
* **Give rise to (Formal Three-part):** Producing or causing new intellectual theories, doubts, or phenomena.
  * *"The unexpected experimental anomalies **gave rise to** a flurry of rival theoretical models."*

## 13. Semantic Domain 12: Cessation, Abandonment & Postponement

Terminating, winding down, or delaying actions and projects.

```
                    TERMINATION & DELAY
  [Definitive Cancellation]   [Phased Wind-Down]    [Postponement / Delay]
  call off                     wind up / phase out   put off
```

### Core Entries
* **Call off (Separable):** Canceling an event, strike, search, or military operation that had already been scheduled or begun.
  * *"Due to torrential monsoons, the mountaineers were forced to **call off** the summit assault."*
* **Put off (Separable):** Postponing or delaying an appointment or obligation to a later date.
  * *"Do not **put off** filing your foreign tax disclosure until the final statutory deadline."*
* **Wind up (Separable & Intransitive):**
  1. *Liquidating / Closing an entity:* Bringing a business, project, or meeting to an orderly close (*"The liquidator was appointed to **wind up** the insolvent airline"*).
  2. *Ending up in a state/place:* Finding oneself in an unintended condition (*"If you persist with this reckless speculation, you will **wind up** bankrupt"*).
* **Phase out (Separable):** Gradually, systematically withdrawing or eliminating a product, fuel, or policy over time.
  * *"The European Union agreed to **phase out** internal combustion passenger vehicles by 2035."*

## 14. Semantic Domain 13: Compensation, Reconciliation & Restitution

Restoring balance, compensating for past losses, or healing fractured interpersonal relationships.

```
                    RECONCILIATION & COMPENSATION
  [Compensating for Deficits]                 [Restoring Personal Harmony]
  make up for                                 make up with / patch up
```

### Core Entries
* **Make up for (Three-part Inseparable):** Providing something valuable to compensate for, offset, or atone for a loss, error, or deficiency.
  * *"Her extraordinary work ethic and structural discipline **made up for** her lack of prior banking experience."*
* **Make up with (Three-part Inseparable):** Reconciling and restoring friendly relations after a bitter quarrel or estrangement.
  * *"After a decade of icy silence, the two founding partners finally **made up with** each other."*
* **Patch up (Separable):** Hastily or temporarily repairing a damaged relationship or a flawed plan.
  * *"The diplomats managed to **patch up** a fragile ceasefire before the winter snows set in."*

### Comprehensive Explanatory Answer Key

* **1. (B) Look into** — Formal, systemic, or official investigation of an allegation, crime, or institutional failure is expressed by *look into*. *(A) Look over* means a quick, superficial check; *(C) Look up* means checking reference material.
* **2. (C) Made up for** — In the sense of compensating or counterbalancing a deficiency or deficit.
* **3. (B) Springboard** — Used in its propulsive metaphorical sense: an active launchpad that propels future expansion.
* **4. (A) Look over** — Reviewing a document rapidly to spot obvious errors or assess quality. *(B) Overlook* is a dangerous distractor meaning to fail to notice or to intentionally ignore an infraction.
* **5. (B) Look down on** — Treating subordinates or colleagues with condescension or social scorn.
* **6. (B) Look forward to meeting** — The grammatical rule is absolute: in *look forward to*, the particle *to* is a preposition, mandating a **gerund (-ing)** (*meeting*), not a bare infinitive.
* **7. (A) Guard against** — Taking strategic, preventive countermeasures against a systemic disaster.
* **8. (B) Came across** — Discovering anomalous evidence or hidden entries through serendipitous, unplanned encounter.
