1. The Spatial Architecture of “Put”

Originating in Old English putian (to push, thrust, place), put fundamentally describes the deliberate placement of an object into physical or conceptual space. In phrasal combinations, it governs temporal deferral, psychological tolerance, intellectual assertion, and authoritarian suppression.

[ Spectrum 1: Postponement vs. Acceleration ] ──> put off             <──> expedite / bring forward
[ Spectrum 2: Forbearance vs. Repudiation ]    ──> put up with        <──> reject / repudiate
[ Spectrum 3: Intellectual Assertion ]         ──> put forth/forward  <──> retract / withhold
[ Spectrum 4: Disparagement vs. Elevation ]    ──> put down           <──> extol / elevate
[ Spectrum 5: Imposition of Ordeal ]          ──> put through        <──> spare / shield

2. Spectrum 1: Temporal Deferral vs. Acceleration

A. Put Off

  • Sense 1 (Postponement): To delay an event, meeting, or decision to a later time or date.
    • Single-word Equivalent: Postpone, defer, adjourn, delay.
    • Polar Opposite: Expedite, accelerate, bring forward.
    • Example: “The engineering director decided to put off the infrastructure migration until after the fourth-quarter shopping rush.”
    • Idiom on Spectrum: Kick the can down the road.
  • Sense 2 (Aversion / Disgust): To cause someone to feel disgust, dislike, or disinterest.
    • Single-word Equivalent: Repel, alienate, discourage.
    • Example: “The abrasive, condescending tone of the technical documentation put off potential contributors.”

3. Spectrum 2: Forbearance and Psychological Tolerance

A. Put Up With

  • Core Definition: To endure, tolerate, or bear an unpleasant, irritating, or difficult person or situation without actively complaining.
    • Single-word Equivalent: Tolerate, brook, endure, countenance.
    • Polar Opposite: Reject, repudiate, dispute, refuse to countenance.
    • Example: “The lead researcher refused to put up with chronic sloppy documentation from the contracting agency.”
    • Idiom on Spectrum: Grin and bear it / swallow a bitter pill.

4. Spectrum 3: Intellectual Assertion and Proposal

A. Put Forward & Put Forth

  • Core Definition: To submit, propose, or state an idea, hypothesis, theorem, or candidate for formal consideration or debate.
    • Single-word Equivalent: Propose, advance, submit, postulate, propound.
    • Polar Opposite: Retract, withdraw, suppress.
    • Example: “In his 1905 paper, Einstein put forward the radical postulate that the speed of light is invariant across all inertial frames.”

B. Put Across

  • Core Definition: To communicate, express, or explain an idea persuasively and effectively to an audience.
    • Single-word Equivalent: Convey, articulate, elucidate.
    • Example: “The defense attorney put across his closing argument with devastating rhetorical clarity.”

5. Spectrum 4: Disparagement and Suppression

A. Put Down

  • Sense 1 (Disparagement): To belittle, criticize, or humiliate someone in front of others.
    • Single-word Equivalent: Disparage, demean, belittle, denigrate.
    • Polar Opposite: Extol, praise, laud, elevate.
    • Example: “A toxic manager constantly puts down junior associates rather than coaching them through errors.”
  • Sense 2 (Insurrection Suppression): To suppress, quash, or crush a rebellion or strike by force (“the imperial guard put down the peasant uprising”).

B. Put Out

  • Sense 1 (Extinguishment): Extinguish a fire or cigarette (“put out the blaze”).
  • Sense 2 (Inconvenience): Cause trouble or inconvenience to someone (“I don’t want to put you out, but could you review this pull request?”).

C. Put Through

  • Core Meaning: To cause someone to undergo or experience a severe trial, ordeal, or painful transition.
    • Idiom on Spectrum: Put someone through the wringer / put through the paces.
    • Example: “The candidate was put through six grueling hours of whiteboard algorithms and system architecture interviews.”
