Introduction: The Restless Verb

Toss is one of English’s most kinetic verbs. At its core it means to throw lightly or carelessly, but its phrasal extensions reach into conversation (toss around ideas), disposal (toss out), ease (toss off), uncertainty (a toss-up), and even indifference (not give a toss). Its etymology is uncertain — probably imitative of a quick throwing motion, appearing in Middle English.

[ THE TOSS SPECTRUM ]

  throw ─► toss aside ─► toss out ─► toss around ─► toss off / toss up
  (light)  (discard)     (eject)     (discuss)      (do easily / decide by chance)

1. Toss (Core Verb)

Phonetics: /tɒs/ (BrE), /tɔːs/ (AmE).

Core senses: * To throw lightly or carelessly: “She tossed him the keys.” * To move restlessly from side to side: “The ship tossed in the waves.” * To flip a coin: “Let’s toss for it.” * To mix lightly (salad): “toss the salad.” * To throw back the head (of a horse, or in contempt): “She tossed her head.”

2. Toss Around

Meaning: 1. To discuss (ideas) casually and informally: “We tossed around a few names for the project.” 2. To be thrown or moved about roughly: “The passengers were tossed around by the turbulence.” 3. To be mentioned repeatedly without resolution: “The same proposal was tossed around for months.”

Nuance: The most common modern sense is brainstorming — informal, exploratory discussion with no commitment.

3. Toss Out

Meaning: 1. To discard or throw away: “Toss out anything you haven’t worn in a year.” 2. To eject or expel (a person): “The bouncer tossed him out.” 3. To propose (an idea) casually: “She tossed out a suggestion.” 4. To reject or dismiss (a bill, case, proposal): “The court tossed out the lawsuit.”

Nuance: The sense “to reject/annul” (as of a legal case) is common in news writing.

4. Toss Off

Meaning: 1. To do something quickly and easily, without effort: “He tossed off a brilliant essay in an hour.” 2. To drink something quickly: “She tossed off a glass of water.” 3. To produce casually or offhandedly: “Toss off a few lines of verse.” 4. (British, vulgar) A sexual sense — note carefully and avoid in formal contexts.

Register: Informal. The “do easily” sense often carries a faint note of dismissiveness — implying the task deserved little effort.

5. Toss Up

Meaning: 1. To decide by flipping a coin: “We tossed up to see who would go first.” 2. To be uncertain or evenly balanced (as a noun, “a toss-up”): “It’s a toss-up between the two candidates.” 3. To vomit (informal, chiefly BrE): “He tossed up after the ride.”

Key idiom — “a toss-up”: A situation in which the outcome is genuinely uncertain, with roughly equal chances: * “Whether they’ll finish on time is a toss-up.”

6. Toss Back

  1. To drink quickly: “He tossed back a shot of espresso.”
  2. To return by throwing: “She tossed the ball back.”
  3. To throw back (the head): “He tossed back his head and laughed.”

7. Toss Aside, Toss In, Toss On

Phrasal Verb Meaning
Toss aside To discard or reject (see the companion article on the aside family)
Toss in To include as an extra or bonus: “They tossed in free delivery.”
Toss on To add casually: “Toss on a little salt.”

8. The Great Toss Idioms

Idiom Meaning Register
Argue the toss To dispute a (often minor) decision British, informal
Toss one’s hat in the ring To enter a competition or contest (esp. politics) Informal
Toss and turn To sleep badly, restlessly Neutral
A toss-up An outcome with equal chances Informal
Not give a toss To not care at all British, informal
Not worth a toss Worthless British, informal
Toss a coin To decide by chance Neutral
Win/lose the toss The coin-toss decision at the start of a match Sports

Note: Several British idioms (argue the toss, not give a toss, not worth a toss) are vulgar-adjacent in origin but mild in everyday British use; they should be avoided in formal writing.

10. Common Errors

❌ “It’s a toss off between them.” → ✅ “It’s a toss-up between them.” ❌ “They tossed the idea about around.” → ✅ “They tossed the idea around.” ❌ “The judge tossed away the case.” → ✅ “The judge tossed out the case.” ❌ “They tossed a coin up to decide.” → ✅ “They tossed a coin / tossed up to decide.”

11. Diagnostic Synthesis

“The committee tossed around a dozen proposals before someone tossed out a radical one. It was, everyone agreed, a toss-up whether it would work. The chair, unimpressed, tossed off a counter-argument, tossed in a modest budget, and tossed back her coffee — and by a coin toss, the decision was made.”

12. Knowledge Retention Quiz

1. What does “toss around” most commonly mean? A) To throw a ball back and forth B) To discuss ideas casually C) To reject a proposal D) To drink quickly

2. What does “a toss-up” mean? A) A discarded object B) An outcome with roughly equal chances C) A quick meal D) A formal vote

3. Which idiom means “to enter a competition”? A) argue the toss B) toss and turn C) toss one’s hat in the ring D) toss off

4. “He tossed off a brilliant essay.” What does this mean? A) He threw the essay away. B) He wrote it quickly and easily. C) He rejected the essay. D) He discussed the essay.

5. In British English, “I don’t give a toss” means: A) I don’t care at all. B) I don’t understand. C) I don’t have money. D) I don’t want to throw.

Answer Key & Rationale

  1. B. Toss around usually means to discuss ideas informally.
  2. B. A toss-up describes a genuinely uncertain, evenly balanced outcome.
  3. C. Toss one’s hat in the ring means to enter a contest.
  4. B. Toss off means to do something quickly and effortlessly.
  5. A. Not give a toss (BrE) means not to care at all.

## 10. Common Errors

> ❌ *"It's a toss off between them."* → ✅ *"It's a **toss-up** between them."*
