Introduction

The present simple is the workhorse of English. It appears in more sentences than any other tense, yet it rarely describes an action happening right now. Instead, it expresses what is habitual, permanent, general, or scheduled — the default backdrop of reality against which more specific tenses operate. Mastering the present simple means learning not just its form, but the precise territory it occupies on the temporal map.

Formation

Affirmative Negative Interrogative
I / you / we / they I work I do not (don’t) work Do I work?
he / she / it She works She does not (doesn’t) work Does she work?

The only morphological variation is the third-person singular -s, which carries the entire tense marking of the present simple.

Spelling Rules for the Third-Person -s

  • Most verbs add -s: work → works, play → plays.
  • Verbs ending in -s, -sh, -ch, -x, -z, -o add -es: pass → passes, watch → watches, fix → fixes, go → goes.
  • Verbs ending in a consonant + y change to -ies: study → studies, carry → carries. Vowel + y simply adds -s: play → plays.
  • Irregulars: have → has, be → is, do → does.

Core Uses

1. Habits and Routines

  • “She cycles to work every morning.”
  • “They usually eat dinner at seven.”

2. General Truths and Laws of Nature

  • “Water boils at 100 °C at sea level.”
  • “The Earth orbits the Sun.”

3. Permanent States and Facts

  • “He lives in Toronto.”
  • “The museum holds over three million artifacts.”

4. Schedules and Timetables (Future Reference)

Used for fixed, officially timetabled events — even in the future: * “The train departs at 06:45 tomorrow.” * “The semester begins on September 8th.”

5. Instructions, Directions, and Demonstrations

  • “You turn left at the second set of lights, then park behind the bank.”
  • “First you heat the oil, then you add the onions.”

6. Live Commentary and Narration

  • “Messi passes to Alvarez… Alvarez shoots… it’s a goal!”
  • In demonstrations: “Now I take the lid off and pour slowly.”

7. Time Clauses and Conditionals Referring to the Future

After when, after, before, until, as soon as, if, English uses the present simple rather than a future form: * “When you arrive, call me.” (not when you will arrive) * “If it rains, we’ll postpone the match.”

8. Declarations and Performatives

  • “I promise I won’t be late.”
  • “I declare the meeting open.”

Adverbs of Frequency

Frequency adverbs (always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never) sit before the main verb but after the verb be: * “She often works late.” (before main verb) * “She is often late.” (after be)

Emphasis with “Do”

The auxiliary do can be inserted for emphasis or contradiction: * “You never help.” – “I do help!” * “Please do sit down.”

Contrast: Present Simple vs. Present Continuous

Present Simple Present Continuous
Habits and routines Actions happening now
“I drink coffee every day.” “I am drinking coffee right now.”
Permanent situations Temporary situations
“She works at the bank.” “She is working at the bank until June.”
General truths Trends in progress
“Prices rise with demand.” “Prices are rising sharply this quarter.”
Timetables Personal future arrangements
“The film starts at 9.” “I am meeting Sam at 9.”

Nuances and Edge Cases

  • The historic (narrative) present: Narratives can shift entirely into the present for immediacy: “So he walks into the room and sees the letter on the table…”
  • Newspaper headlines: “Minister resigns amid scandal.”
  • Stative verbs: Verbs of cognition, emotion, possession, and perception (know, believe, love, own, seem, contain) normally appear only in the simple present, never the continuous: “I know the answer,” not “I am knowing.”
  • “Here/There” inversion: “Here comes the bus.” / “There goes our chance.”

Common Errors

❌ “He work every day.” → ✅ “He works every day.” (missing third-person -s) ❌ “I am knowing the answer.” → ✅ “I know the answer.” (stative verb) ❌ “When you will arrive, call me.” → ✅ “When you arrive, call me.” (no future in time clauses)

Model Sentences

“Every weekday the analyst arrives at the office before dawn, reviews the overnight markets, and writes a briefing that lands on the partner’s desk by seven.”

Knowledge Check

1. Which sentence uses the present simple correctly? A) She is knowing the answer. B) She know the answer. C) She knows the answer.

2. Why is the present simple used in “When you finish, tell me”? A) Because the action is happening now. B) Because English does not use future forms after “when” in time clauses. C) Because “finish” is stative.

3. Where does “usually” go in “He is tired”? A) He usually is tired. B) He is usually tired. C) He is tired usually.

Answer Key

  1. C. Third-person singular takes -s; know is stative and resists the continuous.
  2. B. Time clauses referring to the future take the present simple.
  3. B. Frequency adverbs follow the verb be.

## Introduction

The present simple is the workhorse of English. It appears in more sentences than any other tense, yet it rarely describes an action happening *right now*. Instead, it expresses what is **habitual, permanent, general, or scheduled** — the default backdrop of reality against which more specific tenses operate. Mastering the present simple means learning not just its form, but the precise territory it occupies on the temporal map.

## Formation

| | Affirmative | Negative | Interrogative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I / you / we / they** | I **work** | I **do not (don't) work** | **Do** I **work**? |
| **he / she / it** | She **works** | She **does not (doesn't) work** | **Does** she **work**? |

The only morphological variation is the **third-person singular -s**, which carries the entire tense marking of the present simple.

### Spelling Rules for the Third-Person -s

* Most verbs add **-s**: *work → works*, *play → plays*.
* Verbs ending in **-s, -sh, -ch, -x, -z, -o** add **-es**: *pass → passes*, *watch → watches*, *fix → fixes*, *go → goes*.
* Verbs ending in a **consonant + y** change to **-ies**: *study → studies*, *carry → carries*. Vowel + y simply adds -s: *play → plays*.
* Irregulars: *have → has*, *be → is*, *do → does*.

## Core Uses

### 1. Habits and Routines
* *"She **cycles** to work every morning."*
* *"They **usually eat** dinner at seven."*

### 2. General Truths and Laws of Nature
* *"Water **boils** at 100 °C at sea level."*
* *"The Earth **orbits** the Sun."*

### 3. Permanent States and Facts
* *"He **lives** in Toronto."*
* *"The museum **holds** over three million artifacts."*

### 4. Schedules and Timetables (Future Reference)
Used for fixed, officially timetabled events — even in the future:
* *"The train **departs** at 06:45 tomorrow."*
* *"The semester **begins** on September 8th."*

### 5. Instructions, Directions, and Demonstrations
* *"You **turn** left at the second set of lights, then **park** behind the bank."*
* *"First you **heat** the oil, then you **add** the onions."*

### 6. Live Commentary and Narration
* *"Messi **passes** to Alvarez… Alvarez **shoots**… it's a goal!"*
* In demonstrations: *"Now I **take** the lid off and **pour** slowly."*

### 7. Time Clauses and Conditionals Referring to the Future
After *when, after, before, until, as soon as, if*, English uses the present simple rather than a future form:
* *"When you **arrive**, call me."* (not *when you will arrive*)
* *"If it **rains**, we'll postpone the match."*

### 8. Declarations and Performatives
* *"I **promise** I won't be late."*
* *"I **declare** the meeting open."*

## Adverbs of Frequency

Frequency adverbs (*always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never*) sit **before the main verb** but **after the verb *be***:
* *"She **often works** late."* (before main verb)
* *"She **is often** late."* (after *be*)

## Emphasis with "Do"

The auxiliary *do* can be inserted for emphasis or contradiction:
* *"You never help." – "I **do** help!"*
* *"Please **do** sit down."*

## Contrast: Present Simple vs. Present Continuous

| Present Simple | Present Continuous |
| :--- | :--- |
| Habits and routines | Actions happening now |
| *"I **drink** coffee every day."* | *"I **am drinking** coffee right now."* |
| Permanent situations | Temporary situations |
| *"She **works** at the bank."* | *"She **is working** at the bank until June."* |
| General truths | Trends in progress |
| *"Prices **rise** with demand."* | *"Prices **are rising** sharply this quarter."* |
| Timetables | Personal future arrangements |
| *"The film **starts** at 9."* | *"I **am meeting** Sam at 9."* |

## Nuances and Edge Cases

* **The historic (narrative) present:** Narratives can shift entirely into the present for immediacy: *"So he **walks** into the room and **sees** the letter on the table…"*
* **Newspaper headlines:** *"Minister **resigns** amid scandal."*
* **Stative verbs:** Verbs of cognition, emotion, possession, and perception (*know, believe, love, own, seem, contain*) normally appear only in the simple present, never the continuous: *"I **know** the answer,"* not *"I am knowing."*
* **"Here/There" inversion:** *"**Here comes** the bus." / "**There goes** our chance."*

## Common Errors

> ❌ *"He **work** every day."* → ✅ *"He **works** every day."* (missing third-person -s)
> ❌ *"I **am knowing** the answer."* → ✅ *"I **know** the answer."* (stative verb)
> ❌ *"When you **will arrive**, call me."* → ✅ *"When you **arrive**, call me."* (no future in time clauses)

## Model Sentences

> *"Every weekday the analyst **arrives** at the office before dawn, **reviews** the overnight markets, and **writes** a briefing that **lands** on the partner's desk by seven."*
