Introduction: Prepositions Are Lexical, Not Logical

There is usually no logical reason why English says depend on rather than depend of, or interested in rather than interested on. Preposition choice after a given word is largely a matter of collocation — historical convention, not meaning. This makes dependent prepositions one of the hardest parts of English to master and one of the most reliable markers of a native speaker.

A dependent preposition is the preposition that a particular verb, adjective, or noun “governs” — its required partner. This reference maps the most important patterns.

1. Verb + Preposition

Verb + Preposition Meaning Example
depend on / rely on be contingent upon “Success depends on preparation.”
belong to be owned by / a member of “This belongs to her.”
consist of / comprise be made up of “The team consists of six members.”
apologize for express regret for “He apologized for the delay.”
insist on demand firmly “She insisted on paying.”
object to oppose “They objected to the proposal.”
refer to mention / consult “Please refer to the appendix.”
comply with act in accordance with “All staff must comply with the rules.”
account for explain / constitute “This accounts for 30% of sales.”
result in / result from cause / be caused by “Negligence resulted in the failure.”
suffer from be afflicted by “He suffers from migraines.”
deal with handle “We dealt with the complaint.”
look after take care of “She looks after her grandmother.”
listen to attend to sound “Listen to me carefully.”
believe in have faith in “I believe in you.”
concentrate on focus on “Concentrate on the task.”
arrive at / in reach (a place) “We arrived at the airport.”

2. Verb + Object + Preposition

These verbs take a direct object and a dependent preposition:

Pattern Meaning Example
accuse sb of sth charge with “They accused him of fraud.”
blame sb for sth hold responsible “Don’t blame me for the error.”
remind sb of sth cause to remember “This reminds me of home.”
provide sb with sth supply “They provided us with tools.”
congratulate sb on sth praise for “I congratulated her on the award.”
thank sb for sth express gratitude “We thanked them for their help.”
prevent sb from sth/doing stop “Illness prevented him from attending.”
warn sb about / against caution “She warned us about the risks.”
deprive sb of sth deny “They were deprived of water.”
suspect sb of sth believe guilty “Police suspect him of arson.”

5. Same Word, Different Preposition: Meaning Shifts

The choice of preposition frequently changes the meaning entirely. This table is essential:

Pairing Meaning
agree with (a person) / agree to (a proposal) / agree on (a plan) consent; concur; settle jointly
care about (be concerned) / care for (look after; like) concern vs. caretaking/taste
think of (recall; opinion) / think about (consider) “What do you think of it?” vs. “I’m thinking about it.”
look at / for / after / forward to direct gaze; search; caretake; anticipate
good at (skill) / good for (benefit) / good with (handling) “good at chess,” “good for you,” “good with children”
made of (material, unchanged) / made from (material, transformed) / made with (ingredient) “made of wood,” “made from grapes,” “made with love”
angry with (person) / angry about (situation) directed at a person vs. a circumstance
arrive at (building, decision) / arrive in (city, country) smaller vs. larger location
responsible for (duty) / responsible to (authority) “responsible for sales, to the board”
compare with (differences) / compare to (likeness) “compared with last year” vs. “compared to a lion”

6. The Most Frequent Learner Errors

❌ “It depends of the weather.” → ✅ “It depends on the weather.” ❌ “I’m interested on physics.” → ✅ “I’m interested in physics.” ❌ “Discuss about the plan.” → ✅ “Discuss the plan.” (no preposition) ❌ “She’s married with a doctor.” → ✅ “She’s married to a doctor.” ❌ “We arrived to London.” → ✅ “We arrived in London.” ❌ “Afraid from dogs.” → ✅ “Afraid of dogs.”

8. Knowledge Check

1. Choose the correct preposition: “It depends ___ the outcome.” A) of B) on C) to

2. What is the difference between “made of” and “made from”? A) No difference. B) “Made of” is used when the material is unchanged; “made from” when it is transformed. C) “Made from” is formal; “made of” is informal.

3. Which is correct? A) She is married with a doctor. B) She is married to a doctor.

4. “I’m thinking ___ your proposal.” Which is correct? A) of B) about

5. Which verb takes no preposition before its object? A) discuss B) depend C) arrive

Answer Key & Rationale

  1. B. Depend governs on.
  2. B. A table made of wood (material intact) vs. wine made from grapes (material transformed).
  3. B. Married governs to.
  4. B. Think about means “consider”; think of means “recall/have an opinion.”
  5. A. Discuss is transitive and takes a direct object.
